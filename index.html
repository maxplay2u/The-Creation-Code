People praise your work and then pay someone else, and four steps show you why.

The consultant everyone liked

A consultant came to me convinced they had a visibility problem. They posted every week. They went to events. They coached people for free to build goodwill, and their phone rang more than most consultants’ phones do. People asked for their advice constantly, and the advice was good. They were told so often.

Then they went and paid someone else. In the past year, at least five people took their advice and did exactly that. Sometimes that person gave them the same advice they had already given for free, and sometimes it was worse. More than half of the clients who did sign with them ended up in a friendship with favors in it, and the favors ran in one direction. They charged what they thought was fair, and next to the value they brought, it was nominal.

They were doing everything the standard advice prescribes for someone who is not being found. The trouble was that they were being found. Everyone who met them walked away liking them and trusting them, and filing them under “good person to know.” Nobody filed them under “the person we pay for this.”

Most stalled growth is an order problem. The effort is real and the sequence is wrong. They had reached for reach before their role was set, so their visibility worked perfectly and carried the wrong read to more people.

The Creation Code™

There is an order to how lasting things come into being. Role, then Resources, then Responsibility, then Reach. Four steps, one direction. Nothing skips a step and nothing scales out of order.

I first saw this pattern in the opening chapter of Scripture, and I will show you where, so you are not taking my word for it. Genesis 1:2 describes the earth as “formless and empty” (NIV). The days that follow answer both words. On days one through three, God gives the earth form: light is separated from darkness, the sky from the waters, the land from the seas. On days four through six, He fills it. Many readers have noticed this pairing of forming and filling. The text itself shows the order: the world is shaped before anything is placed in it.

Each step in the book pairs the business case with a passage of Scripture. You can use the sequence without sharing my faith, but I would be hiding something if I did not tell you where it came from.

Step 1

Role

What is this meant to be?

Role is the honest answer to what you are in the mind of the market: the problem you exist to solve, the authority you carry, and the category you should occupy without negotiating for it. It is not your job title, and it is not your list of services.

Most people skip it because it feels like theory, and there are clients to serve and posts to write. When role is left undefined, the market defines it, using whatever evidence is closest: an award, a service list, the last thing you posted. The market files quickly and rarely revisits, and anyone who has tried to change a category that formed around them knows what that costs.

A message built on a settled role can state something. A message built on an open one can only explain.

Check yourself.
Answer yes or no.
  1. After two minutes on your profile, could a stranger say in one sentence what you do and who it is for?
  2. When people refer you, do they describe you the way you would describe yourself?
  3. Does your most visible proof (an award, a title, a post) point to the category you want?
